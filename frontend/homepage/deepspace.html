Black Holes

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing—no particles or even electromagnetic radiation such as light—can escape from it.

The boundary of no escape is called the event horizon. Surrounding it is the accretion disk, a superheated ring of gas and dust spiraling inward at devastating speeds, emitting violent X-rays before crossing the threshold.

Nebulas

Nebulas are distinct luminescent clouds of interstellar dust, hydrogen, helium, and other ionized gases. They are often described as the "nurseries of the universe".

Within these massive, colorful clouds, clumped matter collapses under its own gravity. As the center grows denser and hotter, it eventually ignites nuclear fusion, giving birth to brand new stars.

Pulsars

Pulsars are highly magnetized rotating neutron stars that emit beams of electromagnetic radiation out of their magnetic poles.

This radiation can be observed only when a beam of emission is pointing toward Earth. Because they rotate at incredible, precise speeds (sometimes hundreds of times a second), they appear to "pulse", acting as the cosmic lighthouses of the universe.
